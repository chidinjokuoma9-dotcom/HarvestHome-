# Nigeria Plans More Than 3 Million Fertiliser Bags as Dry-Season Farming Approaches

**By Chidi Njokuoma**  
**HarvestHome News & Insights | Agriculture & Food Security**  
**Published: 18 November 2026**  
**Location: Abuja, Nigeria**

Nigeria is preparing a major fertiliser intervention ahead of the 2026/2027 dry-season farming period, with the Federal Government directing relevant agencies and industry operators to accelerate plans for producing and releasing more than three million bags of fertiliser to farmers.

The initiative is intended to improve farmers' access to agricultural inputs, support local fertiliser manufacturing and strengthen food production. Vice-President Kashim Shettima gave the directive on 16 November during a meeting with fertiliser-sector stakeholders at the Presidential Villa in Abuja, following approval of funding under the Renewed Hope Agricultural Stabilisation and Productivity Initiative.

According to information released by the Office of the Vice-President, the Fertiliser Producers and Suppliers Association of Nigeria (FEPSAN) said available raw materials could support production of approximately three million bags of NPK fertiliser in the coming weeks. The Vice-President also directed the Office of the National Security Adviser to verify declared stocks and monitor the distribution process. He said farmers in communities affected by insecurity should not be left out.

The Federal Government has also announced further measures aimed at easing food-price pressures. At the presentation of the 2026 Wet Season Agricultural Performance Survey on 18 November, Agriculture and Food Security Minister Abubakar Kyari said the government planned to release grains held under the Bank of Agriculture's Guaranteed Minimum Price programme. He said distribution details would be announced separately and that the fertiliser initiative was expected to make about three million bags available within the coming month.

These are announced plans and targets; they should not be mistaken for confirmation that all the fertiliser or stored grains have already reached farmers or markets. The impact will depend on timely production, transparent distribution, affordability and access for farmers across the country.

## What this could mean for farmers and food markets

If implemented effectively, the intervention could help reduce input constraints for dry-season farmers and support production of crops that contribute to local food supply. It could also create demand for local blending plants, transporters, agro-dealers, storage operators and agricultural service providers.

For consumers, additional production and the planned release of stored grains may help improve supply. However, lower retail food prices are not guaranteed: transport costs, energy prices, insecurity, storage losses, weather conditions and market competition will also influence what consumers pay.

Nigeria's agricultural performance still faces risks. Reporting on the 2026 wet-season survey indicates that flooding, crop pests, erratic rainfall and gaps in agricultural extension services remain concerns even as cereal output is projected to rise. This makes practical delivery, farmer support and resilient storage and transport systems important alongside input announcements.
