Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in Ghana

A practical guide to property checks, land registration, safe marketplace transactions and produce trade in Ghana.

Property and agricultural transactions in Ghana require checks that match the land's location, ownership history and the type of produce being traded. A listing is a starting point for questions, not proof of ownership, quality or legal permission.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Ask for the precise region, district, community, plot or parcel reference, site plan and the full name of the person or entity offering the property.
  • Establish whether the land is family, stool/skin, state, vested or privately held, and ask a Ghanaian property lawyer to identify the proper grantor, customary interests, consents and any competing claims. Do not assume a person described as a family head or agent can act alone.
  • Have an independent licensed surveyor inspect the site, confirm boundaries and compare the site plan with the relevant records. Visit the land and check access, occupation, encroachments, easements and planning restrictions.
  • Use the Lands Commission's official services to establish what search or registration service is available for the specific region. The online service coverage and processes can vary; where an online search is not available, ask the relevant regional office about the correct procedure.
  • Have a lawyer review the root of title, search results, deed, stamp duty and registration steps before money is released. Keep signed agreements, receipts and copies of all correspondence.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Write down the commodity, variety, grade, maturity, acceptable defects, quantity and unit of measurement before agreeing a price.
  • Agree a sampling and weighing method, packaging, storage conditions, collection date, transport responsibility, rejection procedure and who bears loss or spoilage in transit.
  • Keep batch or lot references, supplier details, invoices and records showing where the produce came from. For processed or packaged foods, check applicable Food and Drugs Authority requirements.
  • For cross-border shipments, verify the current export permit, phytosanitary, food-safety, customs and destination-country requirements for the exact commodity. Ghana FDA publishes commodity-specific import and export guidance, including fresh fruits and vegetables.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.

  • Lands Commission Online Services
  • Ghana Business Regulatory Reforms — Land title searches and regional coverage
  • Ghana FDA — Import and export forms and guidelines
Country-specific limitation: Land registration procedures and the availability of electronic searches differ by region and by the type and history of the land. Confirm the current process with the Lands Commission and obtain independent Ghanaian legal and surveying advice.
