Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in Kenya

A practical guide to land searches, title verification, site checks and horticultural produce trading in Kenya.

In Kenya, a property advertisement does not establish the seller's title or authority. Buyers should verify the parcel through the land administration system and qualified local professionals, while produce traders should confirm quality, traceability and applicable crop controls.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Obtain the parcel number, registry or county location, acreage, survey information and a copy of the title or other interest document from the seller.
  • Where the parcel is supported by the service, use the Government's Ardhisasa platform to search property information and understand the available land-registration services. The Ministry's State Department for Lands also describes an official search certificate used to confirm ownership and identify charges, cautions or restrictions.
  • Have an advocate independently review the official search, title history, registered interests, land rent or rates where relevant, consents, tenure and the seller's legal capacity. A search result should be current and tied to the exact parcel being purchased.
  • Use a qualified surveyor to confirm beacons, boundaries, access, acreage and any encroachment. Visit the land and check planning, permitted use, occupation, access routes and any community or environmental concerns.
  • Use a written sale agreement reviewed by an advocate. Make payments through traceable channels against agreed milestones and ensure transfer, stamp duty and registration steps are completed through the proper process.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Specify the crop and variety, grade, maturity, permitted defects, quantity, unit, packaging and agreed quality standard in writing.
  • Before dispatch, agree sampling, weighing, inspection, pack-house handling, cold-chain or storage needs, delivery point, timing and responsibility for loss or rejection.
  • Maintain farm/source details, lot identification, invoices and traceability records. For horticultural exports, check current Agriculture and Food Authority requirements for the crop, including any applicable exporter licence and consignment export certificate.
  • Check plant-health and phytosanitary certification with KEPHIS and confirm the destination country's import conditions before harvesting or shipping. Requirements vary by crop and destination.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.

  • Ardhisasa — Government land information platform
  • State Department for Lands — Issuance of Search Certificate
  • Agriculture and Food Authority — Horticultural crops information
  • KEPHIS — Import, export and border control
Country-specific limitation: Ardhisasa services and digitised records may not cover every parcel or transaction in the same way. Confirm the parcel's registry, use current official searches and consult an advocate and licensed surveyor before committing funds.
