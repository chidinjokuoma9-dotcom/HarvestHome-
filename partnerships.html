How the process works

  1. Send an enquiry. Email us with your business or organisation name, contact details, location, website or business profile (if available), and the kind of partnership you propose.
  2. Initial review. HarvestHome will review the proposal and may request supporting information or evidence of registration and authority to represent the organisation.
  3. Discussion and checks. If there is a potential fit, both parties can discuss goals, responsibilities, costs (if any), timelines, data handling and how success will be assessed.
  4. Written agreement. No partnership is approved until the scope and terms have been reviewed and confirmed in writing by both parties.
  5. Launch and review. Approved activities begin only after any required checks and permissions are complete. Performance and concerns can then be reviewed together.

Submitting an enquiry does not guarantee acceptance, listing approval, promotion, exclusivity or a commercial agreement. If a proposal is suitable, both parties should confirm responsibilities, any fees, branding permissions, data handling, duration and exit terms in writing. Do not send passwords, payment-card details, PINs, one-time codes or sensitive personal information by email.

Trust and safety come first

We may ask applicants to verify their identity, business registration, authorisation and the claims they wish to publish. Applicants must provide accurate information and comply with applicable laws and HarvestHome policies.

Any advertising or marketplace listing remains subject to the relevant review process. A partnership, verification badge or publication on HarvestHome is not a guarantee of a business's performance, a listing's ownership or authenticity, or any commercial outcome.

Investment and shares: HarvestHome is not currently offering share trading, investment solicitation or investment crowdfunding through this partnership page. We do not accept investment funds through partnership enquiries. Any future investment-related collaboration would require separate legal and regulatory review and an appropriately authorised partner where required.
