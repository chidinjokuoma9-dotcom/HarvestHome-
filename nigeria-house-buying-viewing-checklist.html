Viewing a House in Nigeria: Questions to Ask Before You Commit

Prepared for buyers, sellers and marketplace visitors. General information only; requirements can vary by state, product and transaction.

A house listing gives you a first impression. A careful viewing helps you decide what to investigate next. Whether you are considering a house to buy or rent, do not rely on edited photographs, a short video or a rushed inspection. Make a written record, ask direct questions and arrange professional checks before making a commitment.

Before the viewing: Confirm the full address or meeting point, who will meet you, whether the person is the owner or an agent, and what documents or information you should bring. Tell someone you trust where you are going.

1. Check the outside and the surroundings

  • Look at the roof, gutters, external walls, windows, doors, fences and visible signs of cracking or water damage.
  • Check the access road, parking, street lighting, drainage and how easy it is to reach the property in wet weather.
  • Look for nearby refuse points, workshops, generators, commercial activity, flood channels or other surroundings that may affect noise, smell, safety or daily use.
  • Ask neighbours about recurring flooding, water supply, security concerns, access disputes and major planned works. Verify important claims independently.

2. Inspect the rooms methodically

Open doors and windows, check locks, look at ceilings and corners, and note damp patches, peeling paint, mould-like growth, unusual smells, uneven floors and visible cracks. Ask when the building was last repaired and whether there have been roof leaks, flooding, fire damage or structural concerns. Do not attempt to diagnose structural safety yourself; arrange an independent qualified building professional where concerns arise.

3. Test essential services

  • Water: Ask the source, reliability, storage arrangements, pump condition and who pays for maintenance. If water quality matters, arrange appropriate testing.
  • Electricity: Ask about the meter, wiring history, prepaid or billing arrangements, backup power, generator rules and safety. Have electrical concerns checked by a qualified electrician.
  • Drainage and sanitation: Check toilets, taps, visible plumbing, soakaway or sewage arrangements and how wastewater is managed.
  • Internet and mobile coverage: Check the networks and service providers you rely on at the actual property.

4. Ask for the real total cost

For a purchase, ask about the asking price, what fixtures or fittings are included, outstanding charges, service or estate fees, expected repairs and the costs your lawyer says may apply to completing and registering the transaction. For a rental, request a written breakdown of rent, agency or professional fees, legal fees, caution or security deposit, service charges, utility arrangements and renewal terms. Confirm which payments are refundable and under what conditions.

5. Confirm who has authority to let or sell it

Ask who owns the property and who will sign the agreement. If an agent is involved, ask for evidence of the owner's authority and verify it directly with the owner through independently obtained contact details. For a purchase, have a property lawyer investigate title, ownership history, encumbrances and planning issues. For a tenancy, have the agreement reviewed and make sure the named landlord or authorised representative is clear.

6. If the house is occupied, ask about handover

Ask whether tenants or other occupants are currently living there, when they will leave, what vacant-possession or handover arrangements apply, and how existing utility balances and keys will be handled. Put agreed dates and responsibilities in writing. Do not assume a property will be vacant simply because an advert says it is available.

7. Take notes and compare fairly

Use the same checklist for every property you view. Record the date, address, asking price, property size or layout, visible condition, services, additional costs, questions still unanswered and the documents promised. With permission, take photographs of areas you need to remember. Compare the total cost and practical suitability, not just the headline price.

8. Do not pay under pressure

Do not transfer a deposit because someone says another buyer is waiting. First confirm the property, the recipient's identity and authority, the written terms, refund conditions and the reason for the payment. Use traceable payment methods and retain receipts. Do not share bank PINs, passwords or one-time codes.

House viewing checklist

  • Address, access and neighbourhood checked
  • Roof, walls, ceilings, floors, windows and doors inspected
  • Water, electricity, plumbing and drainage tested where possible
  • Visible defects and promised repairs recorded
  • All fees and recurring costs itemised
  • Owner or agent authority established
  • Title or tenancy documents referred to a qualified professional
  • Occupancy, handover and key arrangements clarified
  • Written agreement reviewed before payment
