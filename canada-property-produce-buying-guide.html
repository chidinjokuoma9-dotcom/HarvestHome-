Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in Canada

A practical Canadian guide to provincial land title systems, property due diligence and fresh produce import requirements.

Canada's land-title systems and property rules are administered provincially or territorially. Food import and produce requirements are federal in many areas, with additional provincial rules possible. Always check the jurisdiction and commodity involved.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Identify the province or territory, municipality, civic address, legal description and parcel or roll number. Ask for the current title, survey or reference plan, tax information and details of any occupants or leases.
  • Use the official land registry for the province or territory. For example, Ontario's Land Registry records ownership and legal interests such as mortgages, transfers and leases through its official OnLand service. Other provinces have their own systems and terminology.
  • Have a local real-estate lawyer or notary (depending on the province) review title, liens, easements, restrictive covenants, taxes, seller authority, zoning, permitted use, strata or condominium documents and closing requirements.
  • Arrange an independent home inspection and review survey boundaries, permits, utilities, water/septic systems, environmental concerns, flood exposure and municipal compliance where relevant.
  • Ask a qualified local professional to explain provincial or territorial rules that may affect non-resident purchasers, foreign ownership, land transfer tax, disclosure, financing or agricultural land.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Write down the produce variety, grade, size, quality, quantity, packaging, temperature, delivery window, inspection method and rejection process before confirming a trade.
  • Businesses importing fresh fruit or vegetables should check whether a Safe Food for Canadians licence, membership in the Fruit and Vegetable Dispute Resolution Corporation, preventive controls, traceability, labelling or other requirements apply. Specific exceptions exist, so check the current rule for the business and activity.
  • Use the CFIA's Automated Import Reference System (AIRS) and commodity-specific guidance to check the exact product, country of origin and intended use. Some products need phytosanitary certification or an import permit.
  • Keep supplier details, origin, lot identifiers, invoices, transport and storage records. Confirm grade, packaging and labelling rules for the commodity and destination province.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.
