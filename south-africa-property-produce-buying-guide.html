Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in South Africa

A practical guide to Deeds Registry checks, property inspections, land-use questions and agricultural produce transactions in South Africa.

South African property due diligence should include a Deeds Registry search and a review of the conditions and rights affecting the property. For produce, buyers and sellers should agree measurable specifications, handling and traceability before delivery.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Ask for the property's erf number and township, farm name and number, or sectional-title section and scheme details. A street address alone may not be enough to identify the correct registered property.
  • Use the Deeds Registry or an authorised DeedsWeb service to check the registered owner and relevant registered conditions, interdicts, contracts, mortgage bonds and sectional-title information. Confirm that the search relates to the exact property.
  • Have a conveyancing attorney review the title deed, conditions, servitudes, bonds, rates and taxes, seller authority and any required consents. For sectional title, review scheme rules, levies, body corporate information and exclusive-use rights.
  • Check zoning, land-use permissions, building approvals, occupation, municipal services, access, boundaries and any environmental or agricultural restrictions with the relevant municipality and qualified professionals.
  • Arrange an independent inspection and, where needed, a survey. Ensure the written offer, suspensive conditions, deposit handling, transfer process and occupation date are explained by the conveyancer.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Agree the produce type, cultivar, grade, size, maturity, acceptable defects, quantity, packaging and price basis before the order is accepted.
  • Set a sampling and weighing procedure, inspection point, storage temperature where relevant, transport conditions, delivery window and process for shortages, damage or rejected goods.
  • Keep supplier, farm or packhouse identity, batch/lot references, invoices and movement records so the source and destination of produce can be traced.
  • For imports and exports, confirm current plant-health, phytosanitary, food-safety and customs requirements with the relevant national authorities and destination market. Do not assume a commodity is automatically cleared because it is commonly traded.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.
