Eight stories shaping Nigeria today

Wednesday, 18 November 2026 · A concise editorial briefing on the economy, public affairs, agriculture, jobs, trade and community safety.

01 · Business & Energy

CBN and NNPC agree to deepen cooperation on foreign exchange and energy funding

Nigeria’s central bank and NNPC Ltd have signed an agreement to strengthen cooperation around foreign-exchange inflows, energy-project financing and the development of oil and gas assets. The announcement points to closer coordination over oil revenue and investment, although the institutions have not yet published detailed implementation measures.

Why it matters

For businesses, energy investors and import-dependent companies, the practical test will be whether improved coordination produces more predictable foreign-exchange inflows, clearer project financing and greater transparency. The agreement itself should not be mistaken for an immediate change in exchange rates or a guarantee of new investment.

02 · Economy

Experts warn that easing inflation is not yet translating into lower living costs

The Centre for the Promotion of Private Enterprise has urged policymakers to connect inflation management more directly with household welfare. Its chief executive, Dr Musa Yusuf, said food, transport, housing, energy and logistics costs continue to weigh on families and businesses even as price-stability indicators improve.

Why it matters

A slower rate of price increases does not necessarily mean prices have returned to earlier levels. Households still need to compare prices and plan spending carefully, while businesses face pressure from transport, energy and storage costs. The policy challenge is to make economic stabilisation visible in everyday affordability.

Source report: The Guardian Nigeria — read the original report

03 · Agriculture & Food

Federal Government announces further measures aimed at easing food prices

The Federal Government says it plans to release stored grains and make three million bags of fertiliser available in the coming weeks as part of efforts to support food supply and reduce farming costs. The agriculture minister also pointed to mechanisation and investment in agricultural processing.

Why it matters

The effect will depend on delivery, distribution and access. Farmers need inputs at the right time and at affordable prices; consumers need the resulting supply improvements to reach local markets. HarvestHome readers should treat announced measures as plans until availability, eligibility and distribution arrangements are confirmed.

Source report: Daily Post Nigeria — read the original report

04 · Government & Public Affairs

Senate moves State Police constitutional amendment to President for assent

The Senate has resolved to transmit the constitutional amendment bill seeking to establish state police to President Bola Tinubu after reporting approval by all 36 state Houses of Assembly. The Senate also called for a separate National Policing Bill to provide the framework for how subnational police services would operate.

Why it matters

The development marks an important legislative step, but it does not by itself mean state police units are already operating. Implementation will depend on the next legal steps and rules covering recruitment, oversight, funding, training, accountability and coordination with federal security agencies.

Source report: BusinessDay — read the original report

05 · Education & Jobs

87,701 complete first TVET cohort as 150,000 more learners enter training

The Federal Government says 87,701 beneficiaries have completed the first cohort of its Technical and Vocational Education and Training programme, while another 150,000 Nigerians have been enrolled for the second cohort. Reported trades include welding, electrical installation, vehicle mechanics, solar installation and fashion design.

Why it matters

The programme’s long-term value will depend on whether trainees gain recognised skills, useful equipment, work placements and paying customers. Job seekers should verify programme information through official channels and avoid paying unofficial agents who promise guaranteed admission or benefits.

Source report: Punch Newspapers — read the original report

06 · Agriculture & Technology

Jigawa introduces digital farmer registry and market-focused cooperative framework

Jigawa State has signed two executive orders intended to strengthen digital agriculture and farmer organisations. The measures provide for a common data framework for a digital farmer registry and a market-oriented cooperative development framework, with the stated aim of improving planning, market access and financial services.

Why it matters

Reliable farmer and farm data can help public agencies target support and plan infrastructure, while commercially stronger cooperatives may improve access to buyers and finance. The benefits will depend on accurate records, farmer participation, data safeguards and transparent delivery.

Source report: TVC News — read the original report

07 · Trade & Markets

Nigeria’s global yam production leadership has yet to translate into major export earnings

A BusinessDay report says Nigeria accounts for about two-thirds of global yam output but has not converted that production strength into the export earnings associated with a previously stated $10 billion annual ambition. The report highlights the gap between farm output and the value captured through export markets.

Why it matters

Export growth requires more than high production. Consistent grading, storage, processing, phytosanitary compliance, packaging, logistics and dependable buyer relationships all matter. Farmers, aggregators and processors can capture more value when they coordinate around quality standards and verified export demand.

Source report: BusinessDay Agriculture — find the original report

08 · Security & Communities

Deadly farmer–herder clash in Jigawa renews concern over rural safety

The Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps said three people were killed and two injured after a confrontation in Garki Local Government Area of Jigawa State. The incident reportedly followed cattle entering farmland. Authorities urged residents to avoid reprisals while security agencies monitor the area.

Why it matters

The incident underscores how crop damage, competition over land and weak dispute resolution can threaten lives and livelihoods. Communities need timely mediation, lawful reporting, protection for farms and livestock routes, and investigations that establish responsibility without collective blame.
