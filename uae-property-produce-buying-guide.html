Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in the United Arab Emirates

A practical UAE guide to emirate-specific property verification, ownership zones and food import checks.

The UAE is made up of separate emirates with their own land and property authorities and procedures. A Dubai Land Department service should not be assumed to verify a property in another emirate. Buyers and traders should identify the emirate and exact property or commodity first.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Confirm the emirate, community, plot or unit number, project, developer and whether the property is completed, off-plan, freehold or another form of permitted interest.
  • For property in Dubai, use Dubai Land Department's official title-deed verification and property-status enquiry services where applicable. Match the title-deed number, year, property type, owner and property details; independently investigate any discrepancy.
  • For another emirate, contact that emirate's competent land or municipality authority and use its own official verification and registration process. Do not rely on a Dubai search for property elsewhere in the UAE.
  • Foreign ownership rights depend on the emirate, designated area, property type and applicable rules. Confirm eligibility and the precise interest being transferred with the relevant authority and an independent UAE-qualified property lawyer.
  • Review the sale agreement, developer or seller authority, project registration where applicable, service charges, mortgage or encumbrances, completion status, handover condition and registration fees. Use official payment channels and obtain receipts.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Specify the commodity, origin, grade, quantity, packaging, temperature, shelf life, delivery location and acceptance procedure in writing.
  • Before importing food commercially, confirm the applicable federal and emirate-level approvals, food-product registration, import permits, health or phytosanitary certificates, labelling and customs documents for the exact product.
  • UAE food-safety legislation requires relevant official approvals and product registration before trading or importing in the circumstances set out by the applicable rules. The competent authority and process can depend on the emirate and activity.
  • Keep supplier, origin, batch, certificate, invoice, shipping and storage records. Confirm the cold chain and inspection arrangements before dispatch.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.

  • UAE Federal Law No. 10 of 2015 on Food Safety
Country-specific limitation: Property rights, foreign ownership eligibility and registration differ by emirate and designated area. Food import approvals may involve federal and local authorities. Obtain independent advice for the exact emirate, property and shipment.
