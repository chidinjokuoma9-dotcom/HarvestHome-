Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in the United States

A practical US guide to county-level property records, title review, local land-use checks and produce import requirements.

Property records and transaction practices in the United States are largely state and local matters. The county and state where the property is located determine where to check records and which professionals should advise you.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • Identify the property's full street address, parcel or assessor identification number, county and state. Ask for the legal description, current deed, survey and details of any leases or occupants.
  • Contact the county recorder, register of deeds or equivalent office for recorded deeds, liens and other instruments. Check the county assessor for tax and parcel information, but do not treat an assessor page as a complete title search.
  • Use a qualified local title company or real-estate attorney to examine the chain of title, liens, easements, restrictions, taxes, probate or entity authority and the availability of title insurance where appropriate.
  • Check zoning, permitted use, building permits, septic or well approvals, flood maps, access, boundary surveys, environmental issues and any state or local restrictions. Rules can differ sharply between counties and states.
  • Review the purchase contract, contingencies, earnest-money arrangements, inspection rights, closing statement, deed and recording process with a local professional before signing or transferring funds.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • Set out commodity, variety, grade, size, quantity, unit, packaging, condition, delivery temperature, inspection and rejection rules in the purchase order.
  • Keep supplier and lot records, invoices, storage and transport logs, and any required food-safety records. The FDA Produce Safety Rule and other food requirements apply according to the operation, commodity and exemptions.
  • Before importing food, the importer is responsible for ensuring it meets US requirements. FDA explains facility registration and prior-notice obligations for covered food shipments.
  • Fresh fruits, vegetables, seeds and other plant products may have separate USDA APHIS restrictions based on the exact commodity and country of origin. Check the ACIR database and relevant permits before shipment; do not assume a product is admissible.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.

  • USDA APHIS — How to import plants and plant products
  • USDA APHIS — Agricultural Commodity Import Requirements
  • FDA — Importing food products into the United States
  • FDA — Produce Safety Rule FAQs
Country-specific limitation: Property law, recording systems, disclosures and closing practices vary by state and county. Food and plant import requirements vary by commodity and origin. Obtain local legal and regulatory advice for the specific transaction.
