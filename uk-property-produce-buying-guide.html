Buying Property and Trading Farm Produce in the United Kingdom

A practical UK guide to property title searches, regional land registration systems, food-business duties and produce imports.

The United Kingdom does not have one identical property-registration process for every nation. Buyers must use the relevant land register and obtain local legal advice. Food businesses also have registration, safety and traceability responsibilities.

Important: This guide is general educational information, not legal, tax, surveying, food-safety or customs advice. Rules can change and local procedures differ. Verify current requirements with the relevant authority and an independent qualified professional before paying, signing or shipping.

1. Property and land: checks before you commit

  • For property in England or Wales, use HM Land Registry's property information service to review the title register and title plan. The register can show the registered owner, tenure, mortgages, rights and restrictions; title plans normally show general rather than exact legal boundaries.
  • An online copy is not necessarily the same as an official copy suitable as formal evidence. Ask your conveyancer to obtain and interpret the appropriate official documents and conduct the searches needed for the transaction.
  • For Scotland, use Registers of Scotland; for Northern Ireland, use Land & Property Services. Do not assume an England-and-Wales search covers property elsewhere in the UK.
  • Have a solicitor or licensed conveyancer check title, seller identity and authority, mortgage or lender requirements, easements, covenants, planning permissions, lease terms, local searches, flood or environmental risks and completion arrangements.
  • Arrange a suitable survey and inspection. Clarify fixtures, boundaries, access, services, tenancy or vacant possession, completion date, deposit protection and all additional costs before exchange or completion.

2. Farm produce: agree quality and delivery in writing

  • If you sell, store, handle, prepare or distribute food as a business, including online, you may need to register with the local authority. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, the Food Standards Agency says registration should generally be completed at least 28 days before trading; Scotland has separate guidance and local arrangements.
  • Use accurate descriptions and labels, manage hygiene and allergens, keep supplier and customer traceability records, and have a process to withdraw or recall unsafe food.
  • For imports and exports, check the rules for the specific product, origin, destination and UK nation. Plant and animal products may require health certificates, phytosanitary documentation, border controls or other approvals.
  • Before accepting a bulk produce order, specify variety, grade, quantity, packaging, temperature, delivery window, inspection method, rejected-goods process and who carries transport risk.

3. A safer HarvestHome transaction

  • Confirm the seller's identity, authority and current availability of the listing through independent checks.
  • Ask specific questions and keep important answers, invoices and agreements in writing.
  • Agree the total price, currency, fees, taxes, delivery or collection, inspection and refund or rejection process before paying.
  • Do not send money because of urgency, a badge, a photograph or a message alone. Use traceable payment arrangements agreed with the other party.
  • For cross-border trade, confirm payment support, shipping, customs, product admissibility and responsibility for loss before the goods leave the origin country.

4. Official information and further checks

Start with these government or regulator resources. Use the authority responsible for the exact property location or produce shipment, and check whether the page has been updated.

  • HM Land Registry — Search the register (England and Wales)
  • Registers of Scotland
  • Land & Property Services Northern Ireland
  • Food Standards Agency — Food business registration
  • GOV.UK — Starting a food business
Country-specific limitation: The UK has separate property and food-regulation arrangements across its nations. This guide gives a starting point, not a substitute for advice from a solicitor, conveyancer, local authority or relevant regulator in the part of the UK where the transaction occurs.
